Licensing during a government shutdown

The last shutdown ended in November 2025, and the FCC has resumed normal operations. This page is kept because its guidance applies whenever a shutdown happens again. For current timelines, see what happens after you pass.

What a shutdown does to licensing

While the government is shut down, the FCC does not process applications. When it reopens, its systems are heavily backlogged. Applications are processed in order of exam date, so depending on when you tested, it can take several weeks before you see any update in the FCC database.

The FCC’s licensing system is a legacy platform with known stability issues, and only a small number of qualified people can maintain it. After a shutdown, outages and slow processing are likely, and recovering from a system failure can take days.

What you can do

  1. Make sure you have a real FRN. If you took your exam without an FRN, or used ten zeroes, you must get one before your exam can be submitted to the FCC. If you already have an FRN from any FCC license, that is your FRN: you only get one, like a Social Security number. Get it from FCC CORES as soon as possible (steps below), then email it to vetesting@yahoo.com with the subject line formatted exactly like this, using your own number and name: FRN - 0031234567 - First Name Last Name. We update your paperwork and submit your application to the FCC, and that is when the wait for your license begins.
  2. Pay your $35 license fee as soon as the FCC’s system allows, to keep your place in the queue. The FCC emails payment instructions to the address on your Form 605 once it starts processing; check spam and junk regularly. If you miss the email or pay late, the application is dismissed and you have to start again.
  3. Felony documentation. If you have a felony conviction, submit the required documents as soon as the FCC’s systems are back online. This is strictly between you and the FCC; we cannot help or intervene.
  4. Be patient. Please do not contact the FCC, ARRL or us about your paperwork while the systems recover. The FCC’s call center is run by a contractor and may not have accurate information.

How to get an FRN

  1. Go to the FCC CORES website by searching “FCC CORES Login” in your browser.
  2. Click “Register” or “Create an Account.”
  3. Enter your email address and create a password.
  4. After you sign in, select “Register for a New FRN.”
  5. Choose “Individual.”
  6. Fill in your name and contact information.
  7. Submit the form.
  8. Your new FRN appears on the confirmation page. Write it down for future use.

How to pay the $35 application fee in CORES

  1. Sign in to FCC CORES with your email and password.
  2. On the main page, select “Submit a New Filing.”
  3. Choose “ULS Application Fee.”
  4. Enter your FRN and confirm your information.
  5. Select the $35 Amateur Radio License Fee.
  6. Follow the prompts to pay by credit card or bank transfer.
  7. Save or print your payment confirmation for your records.

When to expect progress

  • Once the FCC reopens, licenses are issued in order of exam date, as soon as payment and any verification steps are complete.
  • Your call sign may appear in the FCC ULS database within days, or it may take several weeks, depending on the backlog. If you have paid and still do not see your license after several weeks, it is most likely still in the queue.

Once your license arrives, see getting started.
