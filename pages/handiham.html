Amateur radio for people with disabilities

Amateur radio is for everyone. Whatever your physical ability, hearing or vision, you have a place in this community. Radio is a powerful way to connect with others, learn new skills, and be part of a worldwide network of support and friendship.

We are proud supporters and advocates of HandiHam, a long-standing organization that helps people with disabilities enjoy and succeed in amateur radio. Whether you are blind, deaf, hard of hearing, physically impaired, or live with another condition that makes learning or operating radio more challenging, we are here to help.

Getting started

Amateur radio is more accessible than ever. From voice and digital modes to Morse code, there is something for everyone. If you use assistive technologies like screen readers, speech-to-text, or adaptive keyboards, many tools and radios are compatible or can be modified to fit your needs.

You do not need to have full use of your hands, hearing, or eyesight to be an excellent radio operator. There are ways to operate radios using foot pedals, speech recognition, braille displays, and even sip-and-puff devices. You also don’t need to be able to speak — many operators communicate using keyboards and text-based modes.

Studying for the exam

We can connect you with resources designed to help people with disabilities prepare for the amateur radio exam, including:

  • Audio and braille study guides
  • Screen reader–friendly online courses
  • Deaf-accessible video content with captions and sign language
  • One-on-one support from mentors and volunteers

Taking the exam

If you need help or accommodations for your exam, ask before you book. We will work with you and the volunteer examiner team so you get the support you need to test with confidence.

Can I take a verbal exam?

  • Online: yes. A volunteer examiner can read each question aloud, up to two times. Request this in advance. Reading aloud takes longer, and we allow the extra time; just let us know why you need it.
  • In person: yes. An examiner can read the entire exam to you, and you have up to one hour.

Can someone I know help?

Someone can help you set up before the exam begins, but must then leave: only examiners may be present during the exam. The one exception is a parent, who may observe from a distance when the candidate is under 13.

What if I cannot use the on-screen calculator?

  • Online: only the calculator built into ExamTools is allowed. If you cannot use it, an in-person exam may suit you better.
  • In person: you may bring a basic calculator with no memory or programmable functions. Scratch work goes on the back of the answer sheet.

Can I find out which questions I missed?

No. You are told how many questions you missed, not which ones.

What does an online exam need?

  • An unexpired physical photo ID — see acceptable identification.
  • An FRN from the FCC, before you book.
  • A quiet, private room, not a car or outdoors, with a clear desk and nothing distracting in view.
  • Sharing your full screen and scanning your room over Zoom. If you are not comfortable with that, choose an in-person exam.

We are here for you

You are welcome here. We believe amateur radio is stronger when it includes everyone. We will do our part to support you, answer your questions, and walk with you through every step of your journey — from first learning about radio to earning your license and beyond.

Interested, or need assistance? Email vetesting@yahoo.com and we will connect you with a friendly volunteer who understands your needs. Our examiners are unpaid volunteers, so please allow up to 24 hours for a reply; quicker help is often available in our Facebook group.
